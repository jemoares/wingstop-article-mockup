Can AI make recruitment more human? Wingstop Australia thinks so.

As retailers turn to AI to tackle recruitment pressures, Wingstop Australia is showing how automation can speed up hiring without losing the personal nuance.

With Fleur Brennet, Workday and Jono Spragg, Wingstop Australia

[Publish date]  •  3 min read

Greater recruitment efficiency presents a dilemma. For candidates, automated recruitment can feel impersonal, reducing what should be a human interaction to a series of questions, automated responses and algorithmic decisions.

The challenge for retailers is not simply how much of the hiring process they can automate, but also how to make the experience better for the people going through it.

It’s a question becoming more pressing as the industry faces growing recruitment demands. Retail trade vacancies rose 24.5 per cent in the three months to August 2026, the largest percentage increase of any industry, according to the Australian Bureau of Statistics.

For Wingstop Australia, which opened its first store in 2025 and now has six, with plans for 110, the challenge was particularly acute.

Each new store requires between 40 and 80 crew members. With a new opening planned approximately every six weeks, the business needed a recruitment process that could keep pace with its expansion without overwhelming store managers or alienating potential employees.

Jono Spragg, Wingstop Australia’s Director of Operations, was initially sceptical about using AI in recruitment. Eighteen months ago, he says, he would have “switched off” at the suggestion of a bot managing the hiring conversation.

But years working in hospitality had also shown him how inefficient traditional recruitment could be.

Managers would post advertisements, work through hundreds of résumés and often rely on personal familiarity when deciding whom to interview. Arranging a single interview could take two weeks.

At Wingstop’s planned rate of expansion, that approach would quickly become a bottleneck.

Enter Ranchy

The business turned to Workday Paradox, a conversational AI recruitment assistant, implementing the technology before its first Australian store opened.

Rather than deploying a generic recruitment bot, Wingstop customised the experience around its own brand, creating “Ranchy”, a talking tub of ranch dressing that guides applicants through the hiring process.

The company deliberately made it clear that candidates were interacting with a bot, while tailoring its language to feel conversational and approachable.

Spragg established a guiding principle: make the initial conversation “as automated as possible”, with “human feeling language on top of it”.

The approach was not about removing people from recruitment. It was about removing the repetitive administrative tasks that prevented managers from spending time with candidates.

At Wingstop’s King’s Cross location, the business received approximately 800 applications for 50 positions, completing the process from application to onboarding in just five days.

At Parramatta Square, 1,200 applications arrived in ten days.

But perhaps the most interesting aspect of Wingstop’s approach is what happens after a candidate has been hired.

During onboarding, Ranchy asks each new employee for their uniform size and favourite drink.

When they arrive for their first shift, their correctly sized uniform is ready and their preferred drink is waiting. The team then sits down with the new starter over that drink to begin their introduction to the business.

It’s a small gesture, but one that illustrates how automation can support more thoughtful human interactions.

The bigger question for retailers

Wingstop’s experience suggests the opportunity is more nuanced than simply replacing manual recruitment with AI.

The real value lies in identifying where automation can remove delays, reduce inconsistency and improve the experience for candidates, while preserving the human interactions that shape their first impression of an employer.

For retailers, the question may no longer be whether AI belongs in recruitment, but how to ensure the process still feels human.
